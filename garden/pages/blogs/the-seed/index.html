This should be my first page in my digital garden if I get everything working. My goal is to remove the barrier to creating my own content as much as possible so that I am encouraged to produce more of it and slowly grow my garden!

The system I have set up so far uses a .md file to create a page that is then parsed into .html on my digital garden using a python script. This will enable me to style pages with hierarchical text, bullet points, images, links, etc with minimal worry over syntax since the parser will handle most of the web interface work.

Why?

I recently decided to update my personal website. I looked at what other people had done for inspiration and came across Chester's website. Chester described his website as a "digital garden." I liked how it gathered all sorts of ideas in one place - coffee, photography, coding projects, blog posts, recently read books - that were all central to Chester.

After a quick google, I discovered that digital gardening is real thing! At a high level the digital garden serves to organize your ideas through a web of links - like a personal wikipedia. Recently this idea of creating a digital garden has taken off with the accesibility of blogs, web programming, and apps like Notion and Obsidian. But it turns out that the idea of digital gardening started long before the internet!

The earliest reference to the core idea of a digital garden I have found is the 1945 essay As We May Think by Vannevar Bush, the head of U.S. Office of Scientific Research and Development during World War II. Bush describes a future device called "Memex:"

A memex is a device in which an individual stores all his books, records and communications, and which is mechanized so that it may be consulted with exceeding speed and flexibility. It is an enlarged intimate supplement to his memory.

Bush says the memex could contain books, pictures, newspapers, all in the same place that could be examined at a moments notice. Most importantly, though, the contents are connected via "associative trails" - links that tie two ideas together. At first this sounds like the internet, but the way Bush describes its use case is more personal. Each person might have their own memex, built up over years of use, gathering an ever growing web of interconnected ideas from that persons past. As a side note, this is a super cool article considering its from 1945 and describes futuristic technologies resembling things very similar to what we have today via the internet and AI.

This idea really resonated with me because as of recently, I feel like I am just consuming content from the internet that does not add any lasting value to my life. I couldn't tell you what I was looking at while scrolling on my phone yesterday, yet I probably spent far too much of my time doing so. This problem compounds with the fact that I think as a person my brain is not wired to remember small details of the past.

That is why I am motivated to start my own digital garden. I want to create content that is original or reflecting of the content that I consume, and I hope that by storing it in one place I can revisit my own ideas, creations, and interests in the future and accumulate more knowledge rather than letting my thoughts always fly away.

Goals

I found this post on Maggie Appleton's digital garden to be a great summary of how digital gardens came to be and what their purpose is. She lays out some key characteristcs of a digital garden which I think concisely describe what I am aiming for. I will summarize them here:

  1. Topography over Timelines

    The content in a garden doesn't have to be strictly organized by time or any other linear / categorical order. Instead, it is connected via a graph like structure of links ("associative trails" as Vannevar Bush says).

  2. Continuous Growth & Imperfection

    A garden is a constant work in progress. Content doesn't have to be polished, perfected, or finalized before it is added to the garden.

  3. Playful, Personal, and Experimental

    The garden should be unique and customized to its creator.

  4. Content Diversity

    The garden can / should be host to a variety of content. It is not limited to soley writing, or images, or restaurant reviews.

  5. Independent Ownership

    Building a garden from the ground up gives you the most ownership over its form and future. The availability of AI coding tools makes building a garden using HTML, CSS makes especially accesible.
