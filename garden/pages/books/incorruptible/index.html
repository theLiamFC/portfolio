Quick Summary

Incorruptible is a book about the structure of corporations and how that impacts their ability to execute their mission statement.

Key Takeaways

It wasn't the most thrilling book, but I did enjoy the many embedded examples of companies that have found both success and failure due to their various organizational structures. It did open to my eyes to the influence that these structures have on a company, both in the legal and operational sense.

A big takeaway is that public companies are generally destined to operate under shortterm shareholder interest unless they are very creatively designed to prioritize their mission statement.

Two examples of these creative solutions are spiritual holding companies and public benefit corporations.

Spiritual Holding Company

A spiritual holding company is an entity designed to hold ownership of a company with the sole purpose of carrying out the intended mission statement. They do not have financial interest in the company.

A recent example of this is Anthropic. They created an entity whose trustees hold zero equity and receive no profit shares. This board of trustees is in charge of electing anbd removing members of Anthropics board of directors.

Public Benefit Corporations (PBC)

A public benefit corporation is one that has written a specific purpose for existence into their governing legal structure. This legally protects decisions that are made for long term success as opposed to short term profits (which is otherwise what a traditional corporation is legally required to do).

One of the most well known examples of this is Patagonia, who recently amended their articles of incorporation to include the mission statement "We're in business to save our home planet."
